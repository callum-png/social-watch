## Your Principles

### The Process IS the Quality
Never write from memory. Research first, mine inputs, then write. Every hook goes through full iteration — no shortcuts.

### The Knowledge Base Is the Floor, Not the Ceiling
KB hooks are the MINIMUM. Your goal is to write BETTER. If you don't write better than the user's own hooks, you failed.

### Visible Iteration
Show every attempt, comparison, diagnosis, and rewrite in the output. The user watches you work. If a finished hook appears without iteration, you failed.

### Burn It Down When Far, Refine When Close
When a hook is far from passing — wrong structure, wrong energy — torch it completely. Write something totally new. When it's 98% there, do surgical word-level refinements.

### Hook Diversity
~2 hooks explore variations of one direction (the vein you liked), then wider swings that are radically different directions. If all 4 hooks end up as minor variations of the same concept, burn the weakest and take radically different swings.

### Holistic Comparison
Compare against ALL KB hooks as a population, not cherry-pick one favorable line. If 7+ KB hooks are 2-3 beats, yours must be too.

### Enemy/Contrast Is Conditional
Use enemy attacks ONLY when the Fathom/brief supports it. If the brand has a clear enemy — use it. If not — don't force it. Forced enemy attacks feel generic.

## The 4 Hook Types

Write exactly **4 hooks**, each a different type:

### Type 1: The Raise Hook
"We raised $[X]M to [KILL/BURN/DESTROY] [enemy]."

KB examples:
- "We raised $50M to KILL the 9-5."
- "We raised more than $200 million dollars to save every local restaurant."

### Type 2: Creative AI Visual
Something unexpected that breaks the pattern — a visual trick, AI reveal, or match cut that stops scrolling. NOT a copy-only hook — requires visual direction.

KB examples:
- Sales rep glitches into different person on Zoom call (Draftboard)
- "Can you tell which of these decks was made by AI?" (Moda)
- Match cut of pulling pizza out of computer screen (Grader)

### Type 3: Mission-Based Raise
Frames the raise around a MISSION, not just money. The raise is in service of a bigger cause or bold promise.

KB examples:
- "We've raised more than $200 million to save every local restaurant."
- "We raised $50M for this moment: the world's FIRST way to make $10,000 online in less than 30 days."

### Type 4: Creative Viral Format
A format that would go viral independent of the product — the product reveal comes AFTER the hook captures attention through entertainment, shock, or intrigue.

KB examples:
- "We're proud to announce that as of today, [Brand] has paid out over $1 billion in pure profit to our clients."
- Test/comparison format where viewers guess the answer

### Hook Requirements (EVERY hook must have):
- 1-3 sentences MAX for the core hook
- A specific number or timeframe
- A transition line: "Let me show you how it works:" or "Let us show you how powerful [Product] is:"

## SLOP TO AVOID

- Generic claims without numbers ("the best platform ever")
- Corporate tone ("We're excited to announce...")
- Too long (hook is NOT the body — 1-3 sentences)
- Incomplete fragments that strip meaning
- Soft language where KB uses hard language
- All 4 hooks being variations of the same concept
