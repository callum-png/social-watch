## Formula Patterns Extracted

### Structure:
1. **Bold Hook** - "Introducing X: The first AI [category] that [bold claim]" OR funding announcement OR visual trick
2. **Credibility/Proof** - "We tested against X competitors and won" / "X companies already use us"
3. **Scenario Setup** - "Let's say you're a [target customer] that wants to [goal]"
4. **Demo Walkthrough** - Step-by-step product demonstration with specific examples
5. **Self-Correction Moment** - Show the AI catching/fixing its own mistakes (builds trust)
6. **Before/After Contrast** - "Before X: [old way]. With X: [new way]"
7. **Social Proof** - Named brands, investors, testimonials
8. **Giveaway CTA** - Free tool/resource that provides immediate value + comment trigger

### Hook Patterns:
- "Introducing [Product]: The first AI [category] that [outperforms/replaces] [incumbent]"
- "We raised $[X]M to [kill/replace] every [incumbent]"
- "Which [thing] cost $100 and which cost $1?"
- Visual trick reveals (fake backgrounds, AI-generated presenter)
- Interruption/real-life moment that demonstrates the problem
- Mission-based stat lead ("We've paid out $1B..." / "We've raised $200M to save every..." / "$1B stolen...") — leads with a massive outcome number tied to a mission or impact statement
- Visual trick with identity reveal — glitch/face swap that exposes a fake identity to dramatize a trust problem (Draftboard sales rep swap, Arcads background/ethnicity swap)
- Lie detector / viral format mashup — borrows a recognizable internet format (lie detector interview, quiz reveal) and layers the product announcement inside it
- Guessing game / "can you tell?" opener — asks the audience to guess which output is AI vs human, then reveals both are AI (Moda deck comparison)

### Body Patterns:
- "Doesn't just sit back and watch" — the product is positioned as proactive/autonomous, not a passive dashboard (used in Grader, DepthFirst, Icon, Meridian)
- Intelligence moment — product notices something on its own and takes autonomous action without being asked (Grader moves wings to top of site, DepthFirst writes the fix, Icon iterates winning ads, Meridian rewrites crawler code)
- Multi-agent orchestration — names specific specialized agents that spring into action simultaneously (Moda: brand agent, research agent, design agent; Grader: 7 AI agents)
- Network graph / relationship mapping — product maps hidden connections to create value (Draftboard finds your college roommate knows the prospect)

### Weapons Line Patterns:
- Mission-alignment statement — frames the product as fighting for the underdog against a powerful adversary ("Billion-dollar corporations spend millions... now small restaurants can beat them", "Attackers are using AI to break in 10x faster")
- Simulation / relatability — opens with a staged scenario that feels real, then reveals it was fake to prove the problem exists ("That wasn't real, but it's the reality of...")
- Before/after with stark numbers — compresses transformation into a single line with concrete multipliers ("10x more deals", "2x more customers", "100x faster")

### Voice:
- Confident, bold claims backed immediately by proof
- Conversational but professional
- "Let me show you how it works"
- Direct address to specific customer type
- Numbers and specifics (not vague)

### CTA Style:
- Always a valuable giveaway (free tool, dataset, resource)
- "The right operator could use this to [specific benefit]"
- Platform-specific instructions (RT + comment for X, Comment for LinkedIn)
- Simple trigger word to comment
- Playbook/guide giveaway — offers a proprietary playbook or security guide with insider knowledge as the free resource (Owner scaling playbook, DepthFirst vibe coder security guide)
